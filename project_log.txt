28 Oct 2026 – Yasashwi
What I did: Explored both full datasets and decided the question,
categories, comparison countries and date range.

Question: Did prices in Ireland rise faster than in the rest of Europe
during the cost-of-living crisis, and which categories drove it?

Draft headline: Lower Overall, Higher at Home: Irish Prices vs Europe,
2019–2025 (to be updated once the chart is built)

What I found in the full data:
- CSO CPM15: 136 categories, incl. 7 special groups (codes ZZZ1–ZZZ7,
  e.g. ZZZ3 = Energy). Annual rate of change: 48,960 rows; since 2019
  only 672 of 11,424 values missing.
- Eurostat prc_hicp_manr: 3,533,048 rows (3.4x Excel's limit of
  1,048,576). 45 places (33 countries + groups, incl. 3 versions of the
  euro area). 22,008 missing values. Flags: d = 308,971, u = 17,603,
  b = 1,561, du = 819, e = 184.
- Ireland's values match in both sources (e.g. All items peak 9.6%,
  June 2022), which confirms the two datasets agree.

Decisions and reasons:
1. Categories: All items, Energy, Food and non-alcoholic beverages,
   Housing/water/electricity/gas.
   Why: All items is the baseline. Energy and Housing rose more in
   Ireland than in the euro area; Food rose less. The mix shows a
   contrast, not one simple message.
   Transport dropped: its spike is mostly fuel, already in Energy.
2. Comparison: Ireland (highlighted) vs euro area 20-country average,
   with the other 26 EU countries in the background.
   Why: shows where Ireland ranks among its neighbours, not just vs one
   average. Ireland's overall peak ranks 24th of 27 EU countries, food
   26th, but energy and housing 11th.
   Euro area 20 used because it has fixed members; the other euro area
   series changes membership over time.
3. Date range: Jan 2019 – Dec 2025.
   Why: 2019 = before the crisis, 2022 = peak, 2025 = cool-down. Both
   datasets end in Dec 2025.

Result: selected subset = 9,408 rows (4 categories x 28 places x 84
months), 0 missing values, 8 values flagged "u" (unreliable).

Key numbers (peak annual rate, Ireland vs euro area 20):
- All items: 9.6% vs 10.6%
- Energy: 54.1% vs 44.1%
- Housing etc.: 31.8% vs 23.2%
- Food: 13.2% vs 17.5%
- All items, Dec 2025: 2.7% vs 2.0%



28 Oct 2026 – Yasashwi – Cleaning the CSO data

Started with 244,800 rows and 8 columns.

1. Kept only "Annual rate of change" → 48,960 rows
   Why: Eurostat only has this measure, so both must match.

2. Kept only 4 categories (All items, Food, Housing, Energy) → 1,440 rows
   Why: these are the categories chosen for the story.

3. Kept only Jan 2019 – Dec 2025 → 336 rows
   Why: shows before, during and after the price crisis.

4. Removed 4 columns: STATISTIC, Statistic Label, Month, UNIT
   Why: they had the same value in every row or repeated another column.

5. Renamed columns to: month, coicop_code, category, value
   Why: the old names were confusing (e.g. C01779V04002).

6. Changed the All-items code from "-" to "00"
   Why: 00 is the official code; a dash looks like missing data.

7. Changed month from 201901 to a real date (2019-01-01)
   Why: so the chart orders months correctly and dates match Eurostat.

8. Added short category names and a geo column = Ireland
   Why: short names fit the chart, and both datasets need the same columns.

Final result: 336 rows, 5 columns, 0 missing values.
